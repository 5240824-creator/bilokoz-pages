Approach and sources

Where the method comes from: the Western line (NLP, Integral Eye Movement Therapy, MoM, MindsetMaps, Core Transformation) and the Eastern one (Ho'oponopono, Xu Mingtang qigong), and their synergy.

Position

NLP and life

NLP in its original sense is applied epistemology and the modelling of patterns of genius. New Code shifts the focus from mechanically changing behaviour to managing a high performance state.

Classical NLP grew out of modelling outstanding psychotherapists — Fritz Perls, Virginia Satir, Milton Erickson. The task was not to invent techniques but to describe the structure of what these people did intuitively, and to make it reproducible. My sources here are John Grinder, Richard Bandler, Robert Dilts, Judith DeLozier, Connirae and Steve Andreas.

New Code NLP — what Grinder worked on from the 1990s and taught until 2026 — I studied twice with the author himself. It moves the work from the level of behaviour to the level of state: not installing the right reaction, but assembling the conditions in which it arises by itself. Without an understanding of both areas — the classics and New Code — it is hard to speak of specialising in NLP: they are different technologies with different assumptions about what changes in a person, and how.

An Eastern line adjoins this Western one: Ho'oponopono (Hew Len; the tradition originates with Morrnah Simeona) and the qigong of Xu Mingtang. It is part of the working toolkit on which states and the body are assembled — what helps one think and see differently.

Accordingly, I can demonstrate, teach and consult on all of this — for those who want to master these methods themselves, for example.
