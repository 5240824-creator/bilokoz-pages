Allergies are the subject of my master’s dissertation “Psychotherapy of Allergic Reactions by Psychological Methods”. I work with the psychogenic component of the reaction and with how a person lives alongside an allergy: anticipatory anxiety, avoidance, the tension around the subject. NLP and life

NLP in its original sense is applied epistemology and the modelling of patterns of genius. New Code shifts the focus from mechanically changing behaviour to managing a high performance state.

Expand ↓

Classical NLP grew out of modelling outstanding psychotherapists — Fritz Perls, Virginia Satir, Milton Erickson. The task was not to invent techniques but to describe the structure of what these people did intuitively, and to make it reproducible. My sources here are John Grinder, Richard Bandler, Robert Dilts, Judith DeLozier, Connirae and Steve Andreas.

New Code NLP — what Grinder worked on from the 1990s and taught until 2026 — I studied twice with the author himself. It moves the work from the level of behaviour to the level of state: not installing the right reaction, but assembling the conditions in which the needed reaction arises by itself.

An Eastern line runs alongside the Western one: Ho’oponopono (Hew Len; the tradition originates with Morrnah Simeona) and Xu Mingtang’s qigong.

Neuro-linguistic psychotherapy (NLPt) is today a recognised modality within the scientific community. What popular culture calls “NLP” has nothing to do with the work of Grinder and Bandler. The full account is on the Approach and sources page.

Provocative therapy

Frank Farrelly — an approach in which the practitioner takes the position of advocate for the problem. Provocative humour returns the client to their own strength.

Expand ↓

One of the most underrated approaches in contemporary practice. Farrelly arrived at it through his own clinical work: when rational explanation stopped helping, he began arguing with the client, supporting their worst beliefs about themselves — and found that this gave the person access to their own strength.

It is useful, interesting and paradoxically effective — especially where polite empathy hits a ceiling and the client is stuck in self-pity or intellectual self-deception.
